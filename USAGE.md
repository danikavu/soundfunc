## Using it

- **Space** plays / stops. **⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z** undo / redo. Edits are heard live while playing.
- **Record a jam** — the ● key beside Play, or **J**. Stopped, it plays from the top and records from beat 1; playing, it starts on the next bar. Everything you hear is recorded — every channel, every mutation (R, ⇧R, a node's dice), every slider and tempo change. Press it again to end the take on the next bar (stopping playback ends it right there). The take's card gives **Audio** (WAV of the mix, 16-bit stereo), **MIDI** (notes, with the tempo changes) and **MIDI + automation** (glides and bends as pitch bend), or **Export all** — every file starts on bar 1, so they line up in a DAW. The last take stays under **Export** until the next one. Takes end by themselves after 20 minutes (the audio is held in memory).
- **Plant a function** (left) adds a channel from a preset: Fibonacci, logistic-map chaos, golden ratio, digits of π, Collatz, Lissajous, Euclidean rhythms…
- **Node editor** (bottom): double-click the canvas or press *Add node*. Drag a wire into empty space to add a node that's already connected. Every node shows a sparkline of its output over the pattern.
- **Note Out** decides what you hear each step: `pitch` (mapped onto the key & scale), `velocity` (0 = rest), `length` (in steps).
- **Formula** nodes accept expressions like `3*sin(tau*x) + (k % 3)` using inputs `a b c` and the clock `n x t k`. See the *Guide* tab for every function.
- **Visualizer** (top): *Score* (all channels, drums in their own lanes; **✎** beside the zoom buttons draws the notes in as the track plays — each note grows from its start to the playhead like a pen stroke, glides trace along behind it, and the score wipes clean as the loop comes round (the whole score shows when stopped; the choice is remembered), *Function* (y = f(x) for the selected channel across the whole loop — shorter channels repeat with their loop count `k`, so evolving channels show every pass; **drag across it to hear the curve**), *Scope* (the track to watch: *Pond* — notes as stones with hairline ripples; *Sand* — sand on a vibrating plate: the melody's note sets the plate ringing in its own mode (lower notes, simpler figures), the next note down shades it, the sound's level shakes it and kicks jolt it, and the grains gather on the lines that stay still — Chladni figures; when the music stops the plate rings down into the last note's clean figure; *Halo* — the waveform bent into a ring; *Phase* — a Lissajous figure). **Zoom** with ⌘/Ctrl + scroll or a trackpad pinch (around the pointer), scroll to pan, the − / + / fit buttons, or double-click to see the whole loop again; while playing zoomed in, the view follows the playhead.
- **Top bar — the whole song**: **Tempo** (with ▲/▼ arrows), **Length**, **Key**, **Scale** (44, grouped: none — every semitone; major/minor/pentatonic/blues/bebop; the modes; Japanese; from around the world — double harmonic, Hungarian, Persian, pélog…; symmetric — whole tone, octatonic, augmented), **Harmony** (type a formula, or pick a progression by name from the box beside it) and **Reverb**. **Length** is 4, 8, 16, 32 or 64 beats — *every* channel is exactly that long; the step length you pick fits as many steps as it takes (an irrational step is nudged to land on the beat), up to 1024 steps — or **Free**, where each channel loops on its own (steps × step length, for polyrhythms) and the song is its longest channel. The Score, the Function view and MIDI export use it; the chord progression repeats or carries on inside it. Kits, presets and imports never change it: their channels fit into the Length you set. **Each pass** (in the same menu): *Evolves* (default) — time runs on, so `k`, `bar` and the harmony carry on and every pass can differ; *Repeats exactly* (⟲) — the clock starts again each pass, note for note, in playback and MIDI export alike. Either way the Score and Function views draw the pass you are hearing (the legend says which), and their pitch scale holds steady while playing, only widening if a later pass reaches further.
- **Words**: a **track** is the whole piece (what you save, open and export); its **channels** are the stems — each with its own nodes, voice and sliders.
- **Channel tab → Grid** (right panel): the selected channel's steps × step length and **Octave** (type it, use the ▲/▼ arrows, or ↑/↓ keys; −4 … 9). **All channels**: *Glide all / Unglide all* and *Snap all / Free pitch all* set every melodic channel at once (drums have neither); one ⌘Z undoes it.
- **Sound** (Channel tab, under Voice): every melodic voice is a patch on one synth — unison (up to 7 voices, detuned and spread in stereo), a sub and noise, a low- / band- / high-pass filter with resonance, key tracking and its own envelope (the pluck and the wow), a full ADSR, drive (soft saturation), vibrato, **wow** (a slow tape drift of pitch, shared by every note on the channel so chords waver together), and a chorus and tempo-synced delay on the channel. Voices: Sine (kept pure, for subs and whistles), Triangle, Square, Sawtooth, Temple Bell (struck FM bronze, shimmering), Koto Pluck (a string plucked a fifth of the way along), Mist Pad (soft saws on a drifting tape), Shakuhachi (bamboo and breath), Reese Bass, **Supersaw**, **E-Piano** (FM tines, a little worn), **Brass** (a formant saw section), **Acid**, and your own f(p) — all level-matched. Koto, Mist Pad, Shakuhachi and Brass play their own one-cycle wave: Oscillator → **Own** goes back to it after trying another. **Sauce** — one knob for instant width, shimmer and grit (more unison and detune, stereo width, chorus, a touch of drive and delay, a brighter filter; it only ever adds). **Surprise me** — a tasteful variation of the patch (⌘Z undoes). **Shape** — Oscillator · Filter · Amp · Effects, every setting on top of the voice's own patch; **Reset** returns to it, **Save voice** keeps it. **Sonic Menagerie** — ten finished custom voices, in the Voice menu and as the row of waves under the f(p) formula: *Neon Mantis* (riddim razor), *Glass Cathedral* (shimmer bells), *Bowstorm* (electric violin), *Bronze Titan* (cinematic brass), *Phantom Vowel* (talking bass), *Moon Reed* (haunted clarinet), *Void Engine* (subharmonic growl), *Prism Choir* (celestial pad), *Arcade Plasma* (bitcrushed laser), *Undertow* (liquid neuro). Each sets the wave, the envelope and the whole Shape (filter and its envelope, amp decay and sustain, vibrato, breath, delay time), level-matched to the other voices; change anything from there. **Custom waves** (f(p) Custom) are **locked** against Mutate by default — R / ⇧R leave the wave alone until you unlock it; **Mutate wave** grows a new wave from the current one on its own (how far follows Mutate's Safe ↔ Wild; ⌘Z undoes). **Voice tools** — a row of icons under the Voice menu: **‹ ›** step to the previous / next voice (a melodic channel through your voices, the Sonic Menagerie and the stock voices; a drum channel through the drums); for a custom wave 🎲 *Mutate wave* and 🔒 the *lock*; then 💾 *Save voice* (it turns red, with a dot, and flashes on each edit while the sound differs from every preset and saved voice — saving it, or picking a voice, clears it), *import* voices from a file, *export all* of yours, or *export just this sound*; with a presets folder open they also live in its **Voices/** folder. Drums: Sauce, Tone, Decay, Drive — and punchier recipes (a swept sub kick with thump and click, two-tone snares, airy hats, a clap with a room).
- **Colours**: every new channel (empty, preset, kit, pasted, planted or duplicated) gets a random colour no other channel in the track has. Channel tab: the seven basic colours, then **+** for the full palette of 24 and *Any colour*.
- **Curve node** (Add node → Wave, or the blocks *Sidechain duck*, *Ramp up*, *Swell*, *Bent saw*): automation the Ableton way — points sit only on the corners and the lines between them bend. Click to add a point (snaps to 1/16; Shift places it freely), drag a point to move it, drag a line up or down to bend it (+ starts slow, − starts fast), double-click a point to remove it; ⌘Z undoes. **Repeats** plays the shape again across the loop; **bottom** / **top** set its range, so the same drawing can be velocity (0–1) or pitch (0–7…). It reads the loop's `x`, or whatever you wire into phase. In formulas it is exact: segments of `bend(t, b) = (e^(6b·t) − 1)/(e^(6b) − 1)`, so the Math / JS / Python views and exports match the sound, and the ✂ button writes the curve out as a Formula node. Mutate and the node's dice move heights and bends, never the corners.
- **Curves on pitch** — three ways:
  1. *As the pitch*: Curve → Note Out **pitch**. In Notes mode it is read once a step (stepped notes); in Glide mode continuously (you hear the curve) — *Riser*, *Drawn Melody*, *Bent saw*.
  2. *Added to a melody*: a Math node, melody + curve.
  3. *Inside each note*: Curve → Note Out **bend** (semitones, read continuously while each note sounds — chords bend together). Wire the Time node's **np** (note phase: 0 → 1 across the sounding note) into the curve's phase and the shape restarts on every note, however long. **pressure** (0 – 1) moves the volume inside a note the same way; **slide** (0 – 1, CC74) is the timbre axis many synths map to brightness. Blocks: *Scoop into notes*, *Fall off*, *Blooming vibrato*, *Swell inside notes*. Presets: *Scooped Lead*, *808 Slide*, *Blooming Chords*. The Score draws each note's bend as a line leaving the note.
- **Pitch-bend range** (Channel tab → Export): a MIDI file only carries a bend's position (−8192…8191); the instrument playing it decides how many semitones that spans. Set it (±2 / 5 / 7 / 12 / 24 / 48, default ±24) to match your instrument — or set the instrument (in Ableton, its Pitch Bend Range) to match these. If they differ, bends come out scaled: written at ±24 and played at ±12, every bend is half the size. Glides that slide further than the range start a new note on the way; the range is also announced in the file (RPN 0), for instruments that listen.
- **Two MIDI exports** (Export menu): **MIDI — notes only** (plain notes, tempo & key; glides become stepped notes — a new note each time the slide reaches another semitone; no bends or controllers) · **MIDI + automation** (classic: one MIDI channel per channel, glides and note bends as pitch bend, volume as expression, pressure, CC74) Files: `name.mid`, `name-automation.mid`. *Curves* (Channel tab → Export) sets Simplified / Every sample for the automation export.
- **Note expression in the automation export**: *MIDI + automation* keeps one channel per SoundFunc channel: note bends go on it as pitch bend (exact for one note at a time) and re-centre as each note ends, pressure as channel pressure, slide as CC74. The curves are simplified like glides. (MPE export is not offered for now: it didn't carry more than this.)
- **Curve presets** (Function presets): *Riser* (a two-octave climb that starts slow and gets urgent, with a swelling volume curve), *Pumping Pad* (the chord, ducked on every beat), *Drawn Melody* (a seven-corner curve read as scale notes), *Bent Wobble* (a Reese cut by a bent curve eight times a loop).
- **Harmony that tells a tale** (the box beside Harmony): *Stories* — 8–16-bar arcs (Coming Home, Heartbreak, Sunrise, Night Drive, Descent, The Long Way Round, Epic, 12-bar Blues, Doo-wop); *Grooves* — the changes of a style (jazz turnaround, Rhythm changes, minor ii–V–i, bossa, salsa montuno, funk vamp, disco, deep house, neo-soul, gospel, reggae, mixolydian rock); *Classics*. A progression that needs a scale sets it (the salsa V is major only in harmonic minor). The harmony can change on the beat — `pick(floor(beat / 2), …)` is two chords a bar — and chords are spelled the way the key spells them (B♭ major: B♭ C D E♭ F G A).
- **Rhythm kits** — one finished groove per style, each built with the whole toolkit: a pocket (the snare and the keys sit a touch behind the beat, every hand is humanised, each part swung its own amount), voice-led ninth and thirteenth chords, sidechain pumping from a Curve, and shaped sounds (drum tone, decay and drive, filters, the Sonic Menagerie voices). **Dusty Fingers** (hip-hop: 88 bpm boom bap in F dorian, ghost-note snare, worn Rhodes ninths ducking under the kick, a clarinet answer) · **Cruise Control** (G-funk: 92 bpm bounce, sliding Moog bass, Rhodes ninths, the high whistle) · **Night Shift** (trap & phonk: 140 bpm half-time in C♯ harmonic minor, a sliding 808, rolling hats that climb, a phonk cowbell, choir and glass bell) · **Sunday Pocket** (neo-soul: 86 bpm, ii9–V13–Imaj9–vi9 played well behind the beat, a walking finger bass, strings and a brass swell) · **Fog Line** (trip hop: 78 bpm, a dragged break, choir pad, a slow gliding violin, glass bell in the echo) · **After Hours** (deep house: 122 bpm minor ninths, offbeat bass, echoing organ stabs, a pad pumped on every beat) · **Agua de Coco** (bossa nova: 136 bpm, rim clave, surdo, upright root-and-fifth, nylon guitar, flute) · **Liquid Hour** (liquid DnB: 174 bpm two-step, rolling neuro bass, pumping choir pads, Rhodes and glass arps) · **Phase Garden** (math: a marimba figure in 12 steps against itself in 13 — Reich-style phasing — over 3-, 5- and 7-pulse Euclidean drums and golden-ratio bells). Change the Harmony and every part follows.
- **Style parts** (Function presets): the same basses and keys alone — *Walking bass, Rhodes comp, Nylon guitar, Bossa bass, Montuno piano, Tumbao bass, Slap bass, Clav, Octave bass, Strings, Organ stabs, Pumping pad* — to plant on any track; they follow whatever the Harmony says. The keys are **voice-led**: each chord is wrapped into one octave around the middle of the keys and exactly the notes that would stick out are inverted, so every chord sits in close position and the hand barely moves.
- **Exporting curves** (Channel tab → Export → Curves): glides go out as MIDI pitch bend and expression. *Simplified* (the default) keeps only the points that redraw each curve when a DAW joins them with straight lines — corners stay, straight runs collapse to their two ends, and nothing strays more than 3 cents (pitch) or 1.5 steps (expression); the export message says how many points it saved (a Riser: 2,048 → 135). *Every sample* writes all of them.
- **Stretch** (Channel tab → Grid): play a channel out over more or less time — **÷2**, **×2**, or type any factor (1/16 … 16). Its function keeps its shape (it still reads the same `n`, `x`, `t`, `k`); only where its notes and glide land is scaled, so ×2 is twice as long and slow, ×0.5 twice as fast, at the same pitches. Bars and the harmony follow real time, so a stretched channel still meets the song's chords. A stretched channel shows its factor (×2) in the list. With a fixed Length, a channel stretched past the song plays across passes (with *Repeats exactly*, only its start is heard); with Free, the song grows to the longest stretched channel.
- **ⓘ** buttons hold the explanations — hover, focus or click one to read it.
- **My functions** and **Function presets** (sidebar) fold open and closed, and remember how you left them.
- **Voice** and **Step length** are dropdowns. Voices: melodic, drums and your saved voices, each with a one-line description. Step lengths: straight (1/64 … 2 bars), triplets, dotted, quintuplets/septuplets/nonuplets, free (φ⁻¹, π/8, √2/4 beats) — or type any length in beats (`3/20`, `0.3`, `phi/4`).
- **Pitch range** (the *Pitch* pill in the Score/Function views), on by default: every note you hear and export stays within C1–C7 (or a range you choose — presets for piano, bass, voice…). *Wrap* moves a stray note by whole octaves so it keeps its note name — a function that outputs −8373 still plays a real, in-key note; *Clamp* pins it to the edge; *Off* lets it go anywhere MIDI allows. Drums are left alone. The Function view stays scaled to the range and marks its edges.
- **Clear** (sidebar → Channels) removes every channel at once — ⌘Z brings them back.
- **Glide mode** (Channel → Play as): instead of notes, one voice slides continuously along the curve — `x`/`t` flow smoothly, `n` still ticks per step. Snap to the scale or play free pitch, with adjustable portamento. Exported to MIDI as pitch bend (±24 st) + CC11 expression.
- **f(p) Custom voice**: write one cycle of the waveform itself (`sin(tau*p + 2*sin(tau*p))`); it is turned into an oscillator via its Fourier series.
- **Drums & rhythm**: kick, snare, hats, clap, rim, tom, crash voices. The `Pattern` node takes drum-machine text (`X` accent, `x` hit, `o` ghost, `.` rest). Note Out's `shift` delays hits (swing, flams) and `ratchet` turns a step into a roll; channels also have a swing control.
- **Rhythm kits** (sidebar): one picker, a kit per style, with your own kits on top. Replace the track with a kit, layer the whole kit, add **percussion only**, or add single channels (just the Rhodes, just the hats…) to the track you're working on — at your tempo, or the kit's if you tick “use kit tempo & key”. Double-click a kit in the list to replace the track with it straight away. Hover a kit in the list for its ↓ export icon (and × delete, on your own kits); export downloads any kit — yours or a built-in one — as `<name>.soundfunc-kit.json`; import it under My kits.
- **Harmony** (top bar): the song's chord progression is a function of `bar`, e.g. `pick(bar, 3, 4, 2, 5)` (the Royal Road, IV–V–iii–vi) or `(3 * bar) % 7` (circle of fifths). Every formula reads the current chord as `h`; `L` is the number of degrees per octave. Note Out's `chord` input stacks that many scale tones every `stack` degrees (2 = thirds, 3 = quartal), with `inversion` and `strum`.
- **Randomizer** (Mutate, or `R` / `⇧R` for the whole track): rewrites node parameters *and formulas* — numbers move according to their role (scale degree, period, pulse count, probability), related functions swap, rhythm masks rotate or flip bits, experimental mode grafts new terms — then renders ~40 candidates and keeps the most musical (contour, chord-tone fit, density, distance from the original). The Safe ↔ Wild dial trades consonance for novelty; lock a node to protect it, or a whole channel with the 🔒 after its **M / S** (the one in the header locks or unlocks every channel) — a locked channel is left alone by R / ⇧R, its nodes' dice and Mutate wave.
- **Paste function** (sidebar → Channels): paste LaTeX (Desmos copies it with ⌘C), `\begin{cases}`, plain math with ⌊ ⌋, mod, frac, ≥ — or several lines with helper definitions (`g(x) = …`) and `IF … AND …:` / `ELSE IF` / `OTHERWISE` blocks, nested as deep as you like. “mod” in a sum reads as `(a + b) mod n` by default (switchable); **x starts at** handles functions that begin at negative x. **Paste** (top-right of the box) replaces what is there with the clipboard. **The preview is what the channel will play**: when the function's length (Length × Step) isn't the song's fixed Length, the sheet says so and asks — and the function is cut to the song's Length or runs on to fill it — an import never changes the Length — and redraws the preview over exactly that stretch (its length is shown in the corner). It shows how it read your function — typeset, plus a plot, in a preview that keeps one size (long formulas scroll sideways) — before planting it as a channel; undefined piecewise regions become silence. It reads LaTeX the way Desmos does: Greek letters and subscripted names (`\sigma`, `p_0`, `t_{max}`) are ordinary names; `p = …`, `t_i = …`, `g(x) = …` name the function; every name other than the time variable becomes a **slider** on the new channel (a line like `k = 2.5` sets its starting value, `c = 2a` is written in); the time variable is x, else t, else the one name left over — and you can pick another; trailing conditions like `, \sigma > 0` are noted, not played. Names SoundFunc already uses (k, h, t…) are renamed, k → k_. Choose what x means (beats / steps / the loop) and y (octaves / semitones / scale degrees). See `examples/graphed-melodies.md`.
- **Help in the nodes**: hover any port or setting for what it means (n step, t beats, k loop, strum, ratchet, stack…); the **?** on a node opens a short guide to all of it.
- **Formula editing**: formulas show as typeset math until you click them. While typing, autocomplete offers every function and variable with a description (Tab inserts); **Alt+↑/↓** nudges the number under the caret (Shift ×10, Ctrl/⌘ ×0.1) — live while it plays.
- **Explode / Collapse**: the ✂ on a Formula node splits its outermost operation into a node — Math, Function, Wave, Euclid, Sequence, Steps, Chance, **Compare** (`<`, `≤`, `=`, `≠`, `and`, `or`, `!`) and **If / else** (`cond ? a : b`); any other function call has its arguments pulled out into their own formulas (`rand(n + 16k, 3)` → `rand(a, 3)` fed by `n + 16·k`). Repeat to go deeper. Select nodes and **Collapse** to merge them into one Formula (outside wires become `a b c`). Both are tested to leave the sound unchanged.
- **Sections** node: a piecewise function over time — inputs A–D, lengths like `8 8 16` (beats or bars), drawn as a timeline; also outputs which section is playing and how far through it.
- **Listen** (🎧 on any node): hear one node alone — it drives the pitch (rhythm nodes drive velocity), small ranges are stretched over an octave. Esc stops.
- **Blocks**: ready-made groups of wired nodes in Add node (arpeggio, octave-drop bass, pitch hook, Euclid gate, chord on the harmony, ghost notes, humanize, roll, slow wave, A/B sections). Select any nodes → **Save as block** to keep your own (My blocks, in every track).
- **Sliders**: named constants, shown in a bar above the nodes. Each channel has its own — two channels can both have an `a` with different values. **Alt+S** on a number in a formula turns it into one; rename it and the channel's formulas follow; remove it and its value is written back. The Math/JS/Python views declare them inside each channel's function. (Tracks from before channels owned their sliders: each channel gets the ones its formulas read; the harmony keeps their values written in.)
- **Double-click any slider's knob** to put it back to its default — a channel's Volume, Pan, Reverb, Swing, Portamento, Attack and Release go to the channel defaults; a Shape knob (Sauce, Cutoff, Drive…) goes back to the voice's own setting; the top bar's Volume to 80%; Mutate's Safe ↔ Wild to 0.3; Loops to ×4; a Number node to 1; your own sliders to the value they were made with (one ⌘Z undoes it).
- **∑ Function** (toolbar, or `F`): the whole channel — or the whole track — as one closed-form function. Every node is inlined (a Wave becomes `offset + amp·sine(cycles·x)`, Steps becomes `pick(…)`, Range its clamp formula), shared parts are named in a *where* clause, constants are folded. View it as typeset **Math** (KaTeX, with the clock, harmony and degree→MIDI mapping written out), **SoundFunc** formulas (paste-ready), or standalone **JavaScript** / **Python** that reproduce the engine's numbers exactly (tested bit-for-bit, including the `rand` hash). Copy, or download as `.tex`, `.txt`, `.js`, `.py`.
- **Channels**: **M**ute and **S**olo sit side by side on the left of each channel (hover a name for its voice and steps); the pair above the list mutes / unmutes every channel and solos / clears every solo. Icons above the list: **+** new empty channel, **ƒ** paste a function, × clear all (⌘Z undoes). Hover a channel for **duplicate** (every node, value and slider, as "Name copy") and remove. A new track — or a kit that replaces the track — starts as *Untitled*. The left side is two halves that scroll on their own: the channels on top (their header and keys stay pinned while a long list scrolls), Rhythm kits and Plant a function below — so the channels never leave the screen while you browse.
- **Func View** (the key at the right end of the node toolbar; click again to go back to the nodes): writes every channel you can hear as math in the node board's place — the tune (a drum: its rhythm), or **All** for every Note Out input and the named parts; it follows mute, solo and new or removed channels as you go, scrolls when there are more than fit, and **− / +** (or ⌘/Ctrl + scroll, or a pinch) zoom it from 50% to 300% (double-click the % for 100%). It sits on the node board's own dotted ground; Mutate, Σ and Slider work in both views, and the board keeps its place while you look. Handy for screen recordings: the score above, the functions below.
- **Order My functions** (the ⌄ ⌃ keys at the right of its tools): ⌄ newest first (the default), ⌃ oldest first — by when each function joined My functions: saved, imported, or found in your presets folder (an imported file's own date doesn't count, so what you just brought in is always at the top). Remembered. With a presets folder open, files you drop into its Functions, Rhythm kits or Voices folders from outside turn up when you come back to SoundFunc. Function presets stay in their groups.
- **MIDI → functions** (Export → *Import MIDI…*, or drop a .mid file anywhere on the window): every part of a MIDI file becomes a channel of formulas. The sheet opens on an **overview of the whole file** — every note on a strip of bars — and works nothing out until you choose what to bring in: **drag across it**. **Snap** sets where the ends land: *Bar*, *Beat*, *1/8*, *1/16*, or *Note* (the notes' own starts) — so a part that begins on a pickup or mid-bar comes in from where it begins. Or take **4 · 8 · 16 bars** (from where the selection, or the music, starts) or **All**. Only the chosen stretch comes in, as a loop of its length — a whole song stays light. It finds the grid each part plays on (1/16, 1/8 triplets, 1/32… and its swing), the key (from the whole song) and the mode that fits best, and the chord of every chosen bar (set as the song's Harmony). Rhythms become functions where they are one — `every(n, 4)`, `euclid(n, 5, 16)` — else `bits(0x…, n)` masks; pitches become scale degrees, or degrees above the chord (`h + …`, `wrap(h, -3, 4) + …`) when that is simpler; stacked scale chords become one channel with `chord`, `inversion` and `strum` (voice-led parts read back as the voice-led formulas). **Feel**: *Functions* (the default) writes the feel as functions — each position's level fitted to accents (`0.14 + 0.65 * every(n, 2)`), ghost notes as `chance(…)` with the probability they were played, each position's lateness (`0.2 * every(n, 8, 4)`) and the hand's variation as a `rand()` of the same spread — so loops are as short as the rhythm and every pass differs, as played; *Exact* keeps every velocity and timing as lists, note for note (files exported from SoundFunc come back exactly); *Grid* puts everything on the grid with tidied dynamics. **Follow the chords** and **Every note exact** (notes outside the key make the song use every semitone; off: they snap into the key). **Smart chords** (off by default — off, a part becomes one chord channel only when every chord stacks in thirds in the key, else every voice its own line): the song keeps its key even when a note strays from it (the stray sounds exact through a semitone `bend`), chords are found chord by chord — stacks of thirds or fourths (`stack`), octave doublings set aside, notes played a little apart still one chord — and only what fits no chord (doublings, added tones, strays) goes to small extra lines, as few as there are such notes at once. **Math** (off by default — nothing else changes while it's off): **Sum of sines** writes each melodic line's pitch as a sum of sines over the loop, `c + A·sin(k·τ·x + φ) + …`, fitted only where its notes play and sitting on `h` / `wrap(h, …)` when that helps — a pure function with no list, that Mutate can bend. **Terms**: *Exact* adds sines until every note lands (the engine rounds to the scale degree); *1 · 2 · 4* keep at most that many for a simpler contour, and each channel says what share of its notes still land. **Glide line** plays each line as a glide: one voice sliding from note to note along the curve (only slow harmonics, so it flows), held for each note's length; with *Snap to scale* on the notes are exact and the slides step through the scale, and switching the channel to *Free pitch* makes the whole path one smooth line. The song's **Length** is always yours: no import — MIDI (added or replacing), kits, kit channels, pasted functions, presets — ever changes it; imported channels fit into it (a shorter loop repeats to fill it, a longer one plays its start), and the sheet says which. Each channel wears its part's colour from the overview, and is sorted into a group — **Drums**, **Bass**, **Chords**, **Lead**: click a group's key to leave it all out or bring it back, double-click for only that group, or use **Drums only** / **No drums**; your choice holds when you change the options. Untick channels you don't want (**Select all** / **Select none** above the list), then *Replace track* or *Add to track*.
- **Copy nodes**: select nodes and press **⌘C** (or the copy icon in the node toolbar), pick any channel and **⌘V** (or **Paste**) — or the copy-to icon sends them straight to another channel. With nodes selected, the toolbar shows icon tools (hover for names): explode, collapse, save as block, listen, copy, copy to, lock. Their wires come along, the target's clock is reused, sliders their formulas read are added, and they wire into Note Out only where its inputs are free.
- **Node dice** (🎲 on any node with settings): mutate just that node, as far as the Mutate dial says — harmonious to experimental; the best of 30 tries. Locked nodes don't roll.
- **Saving**: the track's name sits on the right of the top bar, with the save button (⌘S) beside it — a vermilion dot means unsaved changes. The small ⌄ next to it holds your tracks: Save as a copy (⇧⌘S), New track, and every saved track to open or delete. **My voices**: when a channel's voice is a custom `f(p)` wave, *Save voice* (Channel tab) keeps it — the wave, attack and release — to reuse on any channel in any track. Both libraries live in the browser, separate from the open project.
- **My functions** (sidebar → Plant a function — a row of icons, hover for names: 💾 save channel, ↑ import, ↓ export all, 📁 presets folder, lit while linked): **Save Channel** keeps the selected channel as a function — every node with its values and wires, its sound and grid, and its sliders. Click it to plant it in any track (its sliders come with it; it fits the song's Length). **Export** downloads `soundfunc-functions.json`; hover a function to download it alone or delete it — the built-in **Function presets** export the same way (hover one, ↓), as a file Import puts under My functions; **Import** adds functions from either file (names already taken get a number).
- **Export**: MIDI (multi-channel, tempo + key signature, GM instruments), note data CSV (pitch, MIDI number, frequency, velocity, key, timing), or the project as JSON. Projects also autosave to the browser.
